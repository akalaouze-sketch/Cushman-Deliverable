Dallas/Fort Worth Office

Risk to the read
  • Major transactions drove total space availability down, led by the 1.1-million-s.f. sale of HighPoint 2222 and the 225,000 s.f. lease by NXP Semiconductor as the company consolidates.
  • Austin recorded 141,405 s.f. of net absorption, with vacancy landing at 25.4% as the market continues to digest 5.6 million s.f. of speculative office delivered since 2023.
  • Active space requirements increased 30% year-over-year to nearly 5 million s.f. while speculative development reached its lowest level since 2013.

Austin's office market started the year with two major transactions that decreased availability as companies continued to refine workplace strategies. NXP Semiconductors leased 225,000 s.f. across Champions Office Park One and Two, trading its 1.5-million s.f. Southwest campus for modern office space in the Northwest submarket. In another significant deal, an undisclosed buyer acquired Highpoint 2222, a 1.1 million-s.f. renovated property in the Far Northwest submarket that formerly served as 3M's campus. While both transactions reduced office availability by taking space off the market, vacancy rates will not reflect the impact until they move in.

While these deals lowered availability, future vacancy levels may be influenced by the delivery of Waterline later this year. Texas's tallest tower remains fully unleased at 715,000 s.f. Currently, there is 1.8 million s.f. under construction, including Apple's preleased buildings totaling 869,726 s.f.

Despite the mixed activity, the market recorded 141,405 s.f. of net absorption. Vacancy settled at 25.4% from 25.7% in Q4 2025 as the market continues to absorb nearly 5.6 million s.f. of speculative office delivered since 2023. Asking rents have shown limited movement since Q1 2023, with market average asking rates up 1.6% to $53.85 p.s.f. FSG.

Outlook

Looking ahead, leasing activity is expected to remain elevated as tenant demand increased 30% year-over-year from about 3.9 million s.f. of requirements in Q1 2025 to nearly 5.0 million s.f. in Q1 2026. With speculative development at its lowest level since 2013, leasing momentum will have time to absorb existing inventory in the coming quarters.
